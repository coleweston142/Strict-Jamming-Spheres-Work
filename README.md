Strict jamming is the highest level of mechanical stability in hard-particle packing theory.
Strict jamming has the property that the packing resists collective movement of many particles, 
even when combined with a volume-nonincreasing deformation of the system boundary.

The conjectured lower bound for strictly jammed spheres in 3 dimensions has density = . 0.49365

A handful of periodic candidates that exist well below this density have been uncovered.

Process:

  These structures are highly geometric, and with the exception of the lowest density candidate, 
  their 'mesh' or resting layer has been human-designed. These meshes were then analyzed using
  GPT 6.1 and related models to uncover methods of using the natural placements admitted to jam the
  construction and reduce the number of total spheres required upon finding a strictly jammed candidate.
  The proofs for their strict jammed-ness are AI generated.

  Legal placements on this mesh, for the class identified, 
  have row-to-row heights equivalent to (h1) or (h2) which are equivalent in height to the placements 
  admitted by placing one sphere in the natural resting location between 3 mutually touching planar spheres,
  and 4 mutually touching planar spheres respectively.


Properties:

  Two of these candidates admit surprisingly large vertical tunnels. The need for large convex regions at 
  low densities follows naturally from the convexity requirement of local jamming. These vertical tunnels
  are contained within a highly geometric surrounding structure highly reminiscent of high order tilings.
  
  A promising research direction for achieving low densities this reveals is finding methods to create 
  larger tunnels within periodic packings, however this does appear to get significantly more difficult
  past structures containing 'rings' of more than 16 spheres, due to significant challenges in finding 
  structures that preserve the symmetry used up to this point.
